Opening Exercise: Resilient WEFE Project Selection & Prioritization  
Objective:  
Enable participants to identify potential Resilience and WEFE Nexus projects, evaluate 
their integration, and prioritize them for investment based on technical, financial, and 
resilience criteria.  
Duration: 45–60 minutes  
Format: Small groups (3–5 participants), plenary discussion at the end  
 
Step 1: Introduce the Exercise (5–10 min)  
 Explain the goal: ministries often have many water, energy, food, or ecosystem 
projects in planning. The challenge is selecting projects that:  
o Maximize integration across WEFE sectors  
o Improve resilience (climate, resource, financial)  
o Are attractive to financiers (bankable, low-risk, high co-benefit)  
 Show a sample project list (5–7 hypothetical projects), e.g.:  
1. Solar-powered irrigation system in a water-scarce region  
2. Wastewater reuse and energy recovery plant for agriculture  
3. Agro-industrial efficiency upgrade (irrigation + renewable energy + waste 
valorization)  
4. Forest and wetland restoration for flood mitigation  
5. Integrated water storage and microgrid system for rural communities  
 
Step 2: Group Activity – Initial Screening (15–20 min)  
Instructions:  
1. Each group receives the project list and a simplified WEFE Screening Table.  
2. For each project, assess:  
o Sector Integration: Which WEFE sectors are covered? (Water, Energy, Food, 
Ecosystems)  
o Co-Benefits: Potential for climate adaptation, resource efficiency, or 
revenue increase  
o Financial Feasibility: Likelihood to attract finance (high/medium/low)

3. Groups rank the projects based on overall integration, co-benefits, resilience, 
and bankability. 
 
Step 3: Plenary Discussion (10–15 min) 
 Each group presents their top 2–3 project choices and justification. 
 Discuss differences: Why did some groups prioritize certain projects over 
others? 
 Introduce real-world criteria used by financiers, e.g.: 
o Minimum integration measure required 
o Risk mitigation potential 
o Revenue model clarity and cashflow stability 
o Alignment with climate finance and co-benefit metrics 
 
Step 4: Wrap-Up (5 min) 
 Highlight key learning points: 
1. Not all multi-sector projects qualify as WEFE Nexus projects — integration 
must be meaningful and measurable. 
2. Early-stage screening and prioritization improves bankability and funding 
success. 
3. This exercise is a replicable tool for ministries to select high-impact WEFE 
projects.

Lecture 2: Integrating WEFE into Existing Projects 
Objective:  Apply WEFE principles to enhance project economic value. 
Exercise: Retrofitting WEFE for Cost & Value Optimization  
Format: Groups, 60 minutes 
Instructions:  
1. Analyze an existing project case  (e.g., agro-industrial plant with high 
energy/water costs). 
2. Identify: 
o Key inefficiencies in water, energy, food, and ecosystem management 
o Potential integration solutions  (renewables, water reuse, waste-to-
resource, nature-based solutions) 
o Financial implications (OPEX savings, revenue increase, reduced risk) 
3. Prepare a WEFE Integration Plan:  
o Short-term: Low-cost, immediate impact 
o Medium-term: Capital investment, system changes 
o Expected improvement in bankability and resilience 
Deliverable:  Action plan (1–2 pages) with estimated cost savings, revenue gains, and co-
benefits

Lecture 3: Capital Stack & Financing Instruments 
Objective:  Match financing instruments to project type, risk, and stage. 
Exercise: Designing a Capital Stack  
Format: Teams, 60 minutes 
Instructions:  
1. Each group receives a project profile : 
o Public-good infrastructure (e.g., wastewater treatment) 
o Revenue-generating agri-tech project 
o Mixed public-private project (e.g., solar + irrigation) 
2. Design a capital stack : 
o Grants / TA 
o Concessional debt 
o Commercial debt 
o Equity / quasi-equity 
o Blended finance / PPP 
o Green/climate bonds 
3. Justify instrument selection  based on: 
o Project stage (preparation, structuring, implementation, operations) 
o Risk profile and mitigation 
o Financing cost and bankability
