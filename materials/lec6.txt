Who is involved in Climate Finance?
Climate finance networks have been established to allow money to flow to adaptation and 
mitigation projects. There are several key players in Climate Finance
Governments
Governments can provide public money for 
climate finance. At COP 15 in 2009 wealthy 
countries agreed to collectively provide $ 100 
billion a year in climate finance, however this 
target has not been me t.
Multilateral Development Banks
These are international financial institutions 
consisting of member from multiple nations 
with the purpose of encouraging economic 
development in poorer nations. They provide 
loans and grants to countries for projects.International Climate Funds
Governments can contribute money through 
funds including the Green Climate Fund 
(GCF), Global Environment Facility and 
Adaptation Fund.
Private Sector
The Private Financial sector (Banks, Investors, 
Insurers) also mobilise money towards 
sustainable development. The United Nations 
Environment Programme Finance Initiative 
(UNEP FI) supports the private finance sector 
to do this.

SLIDE 44: Case Study 1 –Jordan's 
Aqaba –Amman Water Desalination & 
Conveyance Project
• Project Overview
• One of the world's largest climate adaptation and water security investments.
• Key Figures
• Total investment: ~USD 6 billion
• Water production: 300 million m ³/year
• Pipeline length: 438 km
• Solar power component: 281 MW
• Expected contribution: Up to 40% of Jordan's municipal water needs by 2030
• Financing Structure
• Government of Jordan 
• Green Climate Fund (GCF) 
• International Finance Corporation (IFC) 
• European Investment Bank (EIB) 
• Private sector investors 
• Public -Private Partnership (PPP)

Case Study 2 –Singapore 
NEWater Programme
•Project Overview
•Singapore transformed wastewater into a strategic water resource.
•Key Figures
•NEWater supplies up to 40% of Singapore's water demand
•Target: 55% by 2060
•Operational cost approximately USD 0.08 –0.15 per m ³
•Based on advanced membrane filtration and reverse osmosis 
•Strategic Components
•Wastewater reuse 
•Desalination 
•Rainwater harvesting 
•Demand management
